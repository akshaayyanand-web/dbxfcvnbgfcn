# HomeHealth — Brain Fog Angle: 3 Native Creative Concepts

One angle (brain fog / low energy, reframed from "something's wrong with me" to "something's wrong with my house"), tested through three different **native formats** — each mimics something a real person would actually post or send, not a designed ad. That's deliberate: this ICP (paranoid new parent, "diligent not paranoid" self-image, deep distrust of being sold to) responds to things that look like they were found, not marketed. Built for the Paranoid New Parent persona in the ICP doc — ticking every box, "what haven't I thought of," afraid of being told it's "just parenting."

No polished photography, no logo lockups, no CTA buttons. Each one is a screenshot of something ordinary.

---

## Creative 1 — "The Reminders List"

**Format:** iPhone Reminders app screenshot.
**Why native:** this persona is a checklist-maker by nature (ICP doc: "parents want a system/checklist they can trust"). Handing them one, in the actual app they make checklists in, is the whole insight. The CTA isn't a button — it's disguised as the next to-do, already ticked off.

**Exact spec — recreate as a real Reminders list (or a mockup of one):**

> **List title:** things they said were "probably nothing"
> *6 Reminders*
>
> ● ~~brain fog that doesn't lift, even after sleep~~ *(checked)*
> ● ~~headaches that won't quit~~ *(checked)*
> ● ~~tired no matter what I do~~ *(checked)*
> ○ "it's just early parenthood" *(NOT checked — this is the one that isn't true)*
> ● ~~get the house tested~~ *(checked — via homehealth.co.uk)*

**Build notes:** white background, San Francisco / system sans font, black text, checked items get the native iOS grey strikethrough, unchecked item stays black and un-struck so it visually stands out as "the odd one out." Status bar (time + battery) at the very top sells the screenshot. No AI image needed — this is a pure UI recreation; genuinely easiest to make by typing it into a real Reminders app and screenshotting it.

## Creative 2 — "The Group Chat"

**Format:** WhatsApp group chat screenshot.
**Why native:** the ICP doc names this exact behaviour as a trigger point — "hear something from baby group/parents whatsapp group/school." This creative *is* that moment, not an ad describing it.

**Exact spec — message by message:**

> **Group:** NCT Mums 🍼 · *Steph, Priya, Amara +12*
>
> **Steph** — 10:42
> girls have any of you actually checked your house for this stuff?? 😳
>
> **Steph** — 10:43
> *[shared photo — see image prompt below]*
> found this behind our bedroom window, been putting my headaches down to literally everything else for months
>
> **Steph** — 10:44
> apparently it's standard in like half of UK homes and nobody checks until something's actually wrong
>
> **You** — 10:46
> wait. sending this to Tom right now
>
> **Steph** — 10:47
> homehealth.co.uk — got ours booked for next week

**Build notes:** standard WhatsApp chrome — incoming bubbles light grey/white on the left, outgoing bubble green on the right, small grey timestamps, group name + member count at top. Free WhatsApp-mockup generators (e.g. Fakewhats-style tools) or a quick Figma community file will get you exact chrome; the copy above is the part that matters.

**Image prompt (the "shared photo" bubble — a real discovery, not a stock photo):**
> A slightly badly-lit, unremarkable iPhone photo, taken in a hurry, of a small patch of dark mould spots on a pale wall around a window frame, UK home, faint condensation still visible on the glass above it, phone-camera flash reflection faintly visible, no staging, no people, no text. Square crop. Low production value on purpose — it should look found, not shot.

## Creative 3 — "The Notes App Post"

**Format:** iPhone Notes app screenshot.
**Why native:** delivers the founder-story angle (Theme 2 "I did everything right" + Theme 3 "vindication" from the ICP doc) as a private reflection that leaked out, not a testimonial.

**Exact spec:**

> **the mould thing**
> *Tuesday, 9:41 am*
>
> I keep coming back to this one.
>
> For months I told myself the brain fog was just... having a newborn. No sleep, new baby — of course I felt like I was underwater.
>
> It didn't go away. Not even once she started sleeping through. Headaches. Exhausted no matter what. Days I couldn't finish a sentence.
>
> Got the bloods done. "Everything's normal."
>
> Turns out it wasn't me. Mould, behind our wardrobe, probably since before we moved in.
>
> Built HomeHealth because nobody checks this stuff until something's already wrong.
>
> If any of this sounds familiar — get your home tested. homehealth.co.uk

**Build notes:** white/off-white background (#FFFFFF or #FFFDF7), "‹ Notes" back link in iOS yellow, body in system sans, near-black text (#1C1C1E), grey timestamp (#8E8E93), "homehealth.co.uk" styled as an auto-detected link (iOS blue #007AFF) — that tiny detail sells it. Easiest production path is the same as Creative 1: type it into a real Notes app and screenshot it.

**Optional image prompt (photo attached at the top of the note, evidence not portrait):**
> A slightly badly-lit, unremarkable iPhone photo of a patch of dark mould spots on a pale wall in the back corner behind a pulled-out wardrobe, UK home, visible skirting board and carpet edge, phone-camera flash reflection faintly visible, no staging, no people, no text. 4:5 crop, low production value on purpose.

## Testing & iteration

All three share one angle, so what's actually being isolated is **hook + format** — cleanest read on that is thumb-stop rate (3-sec views or CTR) at small, even spend per creative before optimizing toward cost-per-lead.

Predicted ranking going in, to be disproven by data: **Reminders list** wins scroll-stop (the checklist mechanic is the sharpest hook); **Group chat** wins saves/shares (native to how this exact ICP already spreads this content); **Notes post** wins trust and retargeting conversion (it's the only one carrying the full vindication arc).

Once a winner shows up, iterate its variables one at a time rather than starting over: swap the hook line, not the format; swap the format, not the underlying symptom list — so each test tells you *which lever* moved the number, not just that something did.
